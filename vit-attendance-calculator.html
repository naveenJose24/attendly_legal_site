VIT attendance rule

VIT’s FFCS Academic Regulations set a minimum of 75% in every course. That 25% allowance covers all absences, including medical, personal and event leave. Attendance is checked separately before CAT-I, CAT-II and the FAT.

Your attendanceWhat happens
75% or moreEligible to write the next CAT or the FAT.
Below 75%Marked “Debarred” and cannot write the next evaluation component. Being debarred from the FAT gives an N grade, and the course must be re-registered.
CGPA 9.00+ with no backlogsExempt from the minimum, re-checked before each CAT/FAT. Not available in summer semesters.

Source: VIT FFCS Academic Regulations 5.0. Rules can change between batches and programmes. Your department’s latest circular is final.

Tips for VIT students

  • Each CAT and the FAT has its own cut-off date, so a bad week right before CAT-I can debar you even if you recover later.
  • Because the 25% already includes medical leave, there is no separate pool to fall back on. Plan bunks with room to spare.
  • If you add a course mid-semester, VIT counts attendance from the date you added it.

How the calculation works

Attendance % = attended ÷ held × 100. To find classes you can miss: floor(A ÷ T − H). To find classes you must attend: ceil((T × H − A) ÷ (1 − T)), where T is 0.75 for 75%. Read the full explanation.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum attendance at VIT?

VIT’s FFCS Academic Regulations set a minimum of 75% in every course. That 25% allowance covers all absences, including medical, personal and event leave. Attendance is checked separately before CAT-I, CAT-II and the FAT.

How many classes can I miss at VIT?

Enter classes attended and held above with a 75% target. The calculator uses m = floor(A ÷ 0.75 − H) to show how many more classes you can skip.

How many classes do I need to attend to reach 75%?

It uses n = ceil((0.75 × H − A) ÷ 0.25). Attend that many classes in a row to get back to 75%.

Is VIT’s attendance rule per subject or overall?

It applies to each course. Track each one separately so one weak subject doesn’t catch you out.
