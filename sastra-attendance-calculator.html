SASTRA attendance rule

SASTRA’s examination regulations require 75% attendance in each course for the end-semester exam. Below that, you can still write after paying a condonation fee, with stricter conditions at each lower band.

Your attendanceWhat happens
75% or moreEligible for end-semester exams.
70% to below 75%Eligible after paying a condonation fee for each course.
65% to below 70%Condonation fee per course plus a caution deposit.
60% to below 65%Special classes to make up the shortfall, plus the fee and caution deposit.
Below 60% in a courseComplete an approved MOOC equivalent next semester or repeat the semester. Below 50% cumulative means redoing the semester.

Source: SASTRA Examination Regulations 2026. Rules can change between batches and programmes. Your department’s latest circular is final.

Tips for SASTRA students

  • Each band below 75% costs money, so staying above 75% in every course is the cheapest plan.
  • The 50% cumulative rule is separate. Even with condonation, a semester-wide average below 50% means repeating it.
  • Use the calculator per course: enter that course’s held and attended classes.

How the calculation works

Attendance % = attended ÷ held × 100. To find classes you can miss: floor(A ÷ T − H). To find classes you must attend: ceil((T × H − A) ÷ (1 − T)), where T is 0.75 for 75%. Read the full explanation.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum attendance at SASTRA?

SASTRA’s examination regulations require 75% attendance in each course for the end-semester exam. Below that, you can still write after paying a condonation fee, with stricter conditions at each lower band.

How many classes can I miss at SASTRA?

Enter classes attended and held above with a 75% target. The calculator uses m = floor(A ÷ 0.75 − H) to show how many more classes you can skip.

How many classes do I need to attend to reach 75%?

It uses n = ceil((0.75 × H − A) ÷ 0.25). Attend that many classes in a row to get back to 75%.

Is SASTRA’s attendance rule per subject or overall?

It applies to each course. Track each one separately so one weak subject doesn’t catch you out.
