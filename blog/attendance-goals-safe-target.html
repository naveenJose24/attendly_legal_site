Attendance Goals: Choosing a Safe Target Above the Minimum

Why aiming exactly at the minimum is fragile and how to choose a realistic attendance buffer for your semester.

Updated September 2026 · Attendly guide

A workable starting point

A safe attendance target is the official minimum plus a realistic buffer that gives you room for ordinary disruptions.

Minimum is not the same as comfortable

If your target is exactly at the minimum, one unexpected absence can create a shortage. A buffer gives you options when travel, illness, or schedule changes happen.

The right buffer depends on the number of classes remaining and your real week, not on a universal number.

Make the goal visible

Set a target for each subject, review it weekly, and focus on the subjects closest to the risk line. A clear target is more helpful than a vague promise to attend more.

Confirm the official threshold and any approved absence rules before setting your plan.

Check your institution’s version.

Thresholds, approved absences, and exam eligibility can vary. Use this guide to frame the question, then confirm the answer in your official course or institution policy.
